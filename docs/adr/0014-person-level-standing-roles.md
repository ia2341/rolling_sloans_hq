# What a Person can play is a person-level fact, not a per-semester declaration

Roles are now declared on the `Person`, not re-declared on each `Membership`: a `PersonRole` table records the standing set of Roles a Person can fill, and it persists across semesters the same way `Person` itself does. This reverses the alternative ADR 0001 considered and rejected — "a Person-level profile that persists roles across terms" — but only for this one piece of that profile. `Song` and `Membership` remain exactly as scoped as ADR 0001 describes: a `Membership` is still re-created fresh per `Semester` and still gates who is on that semester's roster and eligible for its attendance/availability machinery, and a `Song` is still never reused across terms. Nothing here reopens either of those.

The distinction ADR 0001 didn't draw, and this ADR does, is between two different kinds of fact that were both living on `Membership`: *whether a Person is on this semester's roster* (participation, correctly semester-scoped — turnover is real, someone who isn't in the band this term shouldn't appear as though they are) and *what a Person can play* (capability, which doesn't reset just because a new semester started). ADR 0001's stated reason for rejecting a persistent profile was the band's real per-semester turnover in "roles and instrumentation" — but that turnover is turnover in *who's in the band*, not in what a returning member's hands already know how to do. A bassist who sat out a semester is still a bassist when they come back; re-entering that fact on a fresh `Membership` every term was busywork standing in for a durable truth, not a reflection of one.

We considered leaving roles on `Membership` and living with the re-entry cost, on the grounds that ADR 0001 had already settled this question and a second look wasn't warranted. That was rejected because the cost compounds specifically for the Person the roster most wants to retain — a multi-semester member — while buying nothing: the semester-scoping that turnover actually demands is fully preserved by `Membership` alone, since a `PersonRole` says nothing about whether its Person is on any semester's roster at all. We also considered a hybrid — semester-scoped roles that default-copy forward from the prior term's `Membership` — and rejected it as the worse half of both models: it still requires a per-semester row an admin must review and correct, while adding a copy-forward step whose behavior on gaps (a skipped semester, a first semester) still has to be designed.

## What changes downstream

- The Role mismatch check (ADR 0002) reads `PersonRole` instead of `MembershipRole` + the viewing Semester. The soft-flag behavior itself — allowed to save, surfaced for an admin to resolve, never a hard block — is unchanged; only where the check looks for "what this Person can play" changes.
- `Membership`'s CONTEXT.md definition narrows to what it has always structurally done — gate roster membership and attendance eligibility for one Semester — dropping the "carries declared roles" clause that now belongs to `Person`.
- A Person's Role-editing surface is no longer implicitly re-scoped by which Semester is being viewed; a returning member's Roles don't need re-declaring, and their Role history isn't fragmented one `MembershipRole` row per past `Membership`.

## Consequences

- A `Person` with no `Membership` at all can still carry `PersonRole` rows — an admin can record what a prospective or former member can play before or after they're on any roster. That's a legitimate state, not a data error: `PersonRole` records capability, and capability doesn't require current participation to exist.
- The schema and data migration moving `MembershipRole` rows to `PersonRole` is separate work (tracked elsewhere); this ADR is the naming of the decision, not its implementation.
- Anywhere that read "this Person's Roles" via their current Semester's `Membership` now reads `PersonRole` directly — a simplification, since Roles no longer need a Semester in scope to be looked up at all.
